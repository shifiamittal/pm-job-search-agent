# Evidence & Truthfulness Rules

Mandatory rules for every role and every resume.

## 1. Evidence must already exist

Do not invent:
- responsibilities
- metrics
- team size
- direct reports
- technologies
- architecture ownership
- business ownership
- customer types
- domain experience
- launch scale
- revenue
- adoption
- savings

## 2. Prefer underlying artifacts over compressed bullets

When detail matters, retrieve the source project artifact rather than relying only on a one-line resume bullet.

## 3. Direct is not transferable

When reusing shortlist career mapping, preserve `capability_fit` and
`domain_context_fit` separately, together with `overall_fit_assessment` and
`limitation_or_difference`. Copy their validated v1.2 values without collapsing
them into one evidence label. Deeper resume evidence is supplemental.

Do not convert adjacent experience into direct experience or downgrade capability
fit solely because domain context differs. Proposed upstream changes require
shortlist revalidation.

## 4. Generalization is allowed; semantic transformation is not

Allowed:
> Built risk-aware ML decisioning products.

Not allowed unless supported:
> Owned healthcare claims decisioning.

## 5. Preserve metric definitions

Do not swap:
- revenue
- spend
- GMS
- cost savings
- projected value
- annualized value
- pilot impact
- production impact

## 6. Preserve population / denominator

Keep clear whether a metric refers to:
- pilot cohort
- full population
- approved users
- active users
- eligible users
- one geography
- global scope

## 7. Goal ≠ result

Targets, forecasts, simulations, and business cases must not be presented as realized outcomes.

## 8. Validation ≠ production impact

Offline lift or simulation is not automatically customer impact.

## 9. Technical fluency ≠ implementation ownership

A PM can define requirements and make architecture trade-offs without personally implementing every component.

## 10. Formal management ≠ cross-functional leadership

Do not infer direct reports from "led" or "worked across".

## 11. Conflicting metrics remain unresolved

List competing versions and sources. Use only a validated or candidate-confirmed version.

## 12. Gaps remain visible

A strong tailored resume may emphasize transferable evidence. It may not erase a genuine gap.

## 13. Use strongest evidence, not every evidence

Select by:
- relevance
- ownership
- impact
- seniority
- credibility
- differentiation

## 14. Preferred evidence hierarchy

1. validated source artifact
2. candidate-confirmed fact
3. current approved resume wording
4. older resume / note with caveat
5. model inference — not acceptable as factual evidence

## 15. Confidence levels

- High
- Medium
- Low
- Unresolved

## 16. Writer may improve wording, not truth

May improve:
- clarity
- concision
- relevance
- terminology

May not improve:
- scope
- metric size
- ownership
- seniority
- domain experience

## 17. Final unsupported-claim audit

For every important bullet ask:
- What source supports this?
- Is ownership explicit?
- Is the metric definition preserved?
- Is the population preserved?
- Is this direct or transferable?
- Did wording imply more than the source says?
- Is a goal being presented as a result?
- Is a validation metric being presented as production impact?
